GOOD TO KNOW

See the whole picture.

The two systems measure nutritional quality differently. Neither can tell you whether a food is right for your overall diet.

FR

Nutri-Score

A five-letter A–E indicator, based on the 2023 revised algorithm implemented in France from March 2025. It considers calories, sugars, saturated fat, salt, fibre, protein and qualifying fruit/vegetable/legume content, with different rules for drinks and fats.

Official method ↗
AU

Health Star Rating

An Australian and New Zealand system assigning 0.5 to 5 stars. It uses separate thresholds for beverages, milk, other dairy, general foods, oils and high-calcium cheeses. The version used here is Implementation Guide v9 (December 2025).

Official method ↗

Where data come from

Food details come from Open Food Facts, a crowdsourced database. Some information may be missing, inaccurate or from a different formulation. Always check the package before relying on the result.

Your privacy

Scans and favourites are saved locally in this browser. Product barcode lookups are sent directly to Open Food Facts. No tracking account, analytics or personal server is used. Clearing site data removes local history.

Important: Ratings calculated by FoodLens are informational reproductions, not official certifications. Missing nutrients or ingredient percentages produce a range rather than an invented exact score. Some foods are not eligible, and HSR category eligibility (especially dairy alternatives) requires checking the product's composition. Products should be compared with similar products, not across unrelated food groups.
